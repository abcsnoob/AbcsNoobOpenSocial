# Governance Model

**Abc's Noob Open Social**

## 1. Governance Philosophy

**Abc's Noob Open Social** is governed by **code, cryptography, and freedom to fork** — not by people, titles, or institutions.

This project rejects:

* central leadership
* ownership-based authority
* corporate-style governance
* political or legal control

There are **no rulers**, only participants.

---

## 2. No Central Authority

The project has:

* ❌ No project owner
* ❌ No board
* ❌ No foundation
* ❌ No steering committee
* ❌ No benevolent dictator
* ❌ No “core team” with special powers

The original developer has **no authority beyond their own contributions**.

---

## 3. Code Is Law (Until Forked)

Decisions are made by:

* writing code
* reviewing code
* running code

If you disagree with a decision:

* you are **not required to comply**
* you may **fork the project**
* you may **run your own version**

Forking is not hostility.
Forking is governance.

---

## 4. Maintainers and Reviewers

Maintainers:

* are volunteers
* have **no permanent status**
* have **no special legal or technical authority**
* can be ignored or replaced by forks at any time

Review power exists **only by community consent**, not by force.

---

## 5. Decision Making

There is no voting system.

Changes happen when:

* code is proposed
* reviewed publicly
* adopted by users or nodes

Adoption is the only form of legitimacy.

---

## 6. Conflict Resolution

Conflicts are resolved by:

1. discussion
2. experimentation
3. forking

There is no arbitration authority.
There are no binding rulings.

---

## 7. Funding and Influence

* There is no treasury
* There are no sponsors with control
* Donations grant **no influence**
* Funding does not buy authority

Money cannot change the rules.

---

## 8. No Emergency Powers

There are:

* no emergency shutdowns
* no override keys
* no rollback authority
* no forced updates

Even in crises:

* no one gains special powers

---

## 9. Legal Resistance

The project is intentionally structured so that:

* developers cannot comply with censorship requests
* maintainers cannot remove users
* no entity can “take over” the network

Any attempt to impose control can only result in forks.

---

## 10. Relationship to the License

This governance model is enforced by:

* **GNU GPLv3**
* absence of Contributor License Agreements (CLA)
* absence of centralized infrastructure

Legal power is replaced by structural impossibility.

---

## 11. What Governance Is *Not*

Governance is **not**:

* moderation
* content control
* moral enforcement
* identity verification
* reputation scoring

Each node governs itself.

---

## 12. Final Statement

> “If you want power, fork it.
> If others follow you, that is consent.
> If they don’t, you never had authority.”
